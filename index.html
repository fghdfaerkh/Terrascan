Territory Scanner · NASA data

What problems TerraScan solves

Farmers have to make sowing decisions without knowing what the weather and soil will do. TerraScan turns NASA data into a clear plan for your field.

“I don’t know what risks my field faces”
Solution: we show water deficit, heat, frost, heavy rain, erosion and waterlogging using NASA, SRTM and SoilGrids data.
“My field is uneven — what will happen where?”
Solution: we split your outline into zones; each has its own terrain, slope, and risk of erosion and waterlogging.
“Which crop rotation should I choose?”
Solution: we compare three options against your priorities and show what each conclusion rests on and what data is missing.
“What, when and how much should I sow?”
Solution: we give you a work calendar and figures for your area: seeds, fertilizer, water, expected yield.
“My calculations will get lost”
Solution: the point, outline and plan are saved to your history under your own name, so you can return to them at any time.

How the indicators are calculated

  • Water deficit: ET0 by Hargreaves–Samani from POWER temperatures for April–September. An index of 100 means precipitation does not cover evaporative demand at all.
  • Heat: number of days with a maximum of 35 °C or higher in June–August. 25 days or more give 100.
  • Frost: number of days with a minimum of 0 °C or lower in April–May and September–October. 20 days or more give 100.
  • Dry soil and waterlogging: share of days in April–September with root-zone moisture below 0.35 and above 0.85 (MERRA-2); for waterlogging, slope and terrain position (SRTM) are added.
  • Heavy rain and erosion: days with precipitation of 20 mm or more (IMERG; 6 days = 100) and slope (15 % = 100) in a 40/60 ratio. This is a simplification, not RUSLE.
  • “Bare” periods: share of MODIS scenes from April–October with NDVI below 0.25.
  • Water retention: available moisture (field capacity minus wilting point) of the 0–30 cm layer from SoilGrids.
  • Field outline: shape, area and perimeter are calculated from the vertices you placed on the map. NASA risks apply to the whole point, not to parts of the outline.
  • Rotation scores and plan figures: seeding rates, water requirements, timing and yields are approximate guides; they should be replaced with calculations based on FAO-56, RUSLE and TWI and checked with an agronomist. Risk thresholds are conventional.
